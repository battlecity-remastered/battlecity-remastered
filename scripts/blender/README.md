# BattleCity DX Three.js assets

Rebuild the industrial models with:

```sh
nix develop -c blender --background --python-exit-code 1 --python scripts/blender/build_demo_assets.py
nix develop -c blender --background --python-exit-code 1 --python scripts/blender/build_mayor_tank.py
nix develop -c blender --background --python-exit-code 1 --python scripts/blender/build_housing.py
```

Run the mayor/housing exporters last to retain their dedicated models. Editable
sources are in `scripts/blender/source/`; exported GLBs are in
`apps/client-ts/public/assets/models/`. Commit both when updating models.
Geometry is grouped by material and animated pivot to reduce draw calls.

One Three.js unit is one original 48-pixel tile. The orthographic camera retains
48 × 48 CSS pixels per ground tile, compensates for its elevation, and reserves
the original 200-pixel inventory sidebar. Buildings occupy three-tile plots;
items, tanks and deployed defenses retain their original one-tile bounds.
The command centre's body occupies its first two rows; the southern **NO PARKING**
apron remains walkable and carries the distressed original-style marking.
The exported tank is approximately 0.676 tile wide. Collision, drops and blast
footprints use the original tile-based gameplay rather than the decorative mesh.

The mineral albedo `apps/client-ts/public/assets/materials/dx-mineral-ground.png`
is an image-generated seamless texture informed by DX assets and the original
in-game reference. It is sampled across four tiles with mipmaps and anisotropic
filtering, plus procedural wear, normal relief, shadows and ambient occlusion.
Lava uses the original map cells with rounded visible banks, steep exposed rock
and a molten surface recessed up to 1.05 tiles. Animated currents, cooled skin,
embers and local lighting are generated by the Three.js renderer.

The DX factories retain twin stacks, inward dispatch arrows and their thirteen
matching products. Research uses a levitating faceted specimen and an induction
scanner. Its front terminal renders the actual item model into a 192 × 192 HDR
texture at at most 10 Hz. Active research shows an amber plasma hourglass and
countdown; confirmed completion reveals the teal item hologram. Chamber rings,
specimens and reactors share the state lighting. The orb factory and orb item
retain violet plasma, cyan reticles, charged filaments and moving gimbals.
Housing uses dark machinery, satin chrome, subdued apartment glazing and recessed
population panels. Mayors have a distinct rounded teal command tank.

The default `/` route is authoritative multiplayer; `?demo=1` is an unlocked
mayor sandbox. Right-click/F4 opens construction, arrows drive, Space/Shift or
left-click fires, U collects, D/G drops beneath the tank, V toggles bomb arming,
B drops an armed bomb, and O attempts city orbing. F2/M opens the map and F3 shows
diagnostics. The sandbox permits self-orbing and reset, and demonstrates the
rocket lab researching/ready cycle. Live actions follow server rules.

Walls and towers unfold on deployment. Bomb effects use bounded fire/smoke pools;
destroyed buildings transfer actual asset meshes to a temporary collapse/debris
rig. Shared source geometry is retained, while owned visual resources are cleaned
up. Terrain chunks, rock/building instance batches, distance/frustum culling,
shared animation clocks and shadow reuse keep the world render bounded.
